1. Overview

Decided A roguelike in the vein of FTL: ships made of rooms, tiles and systems; jumping from star system to star system; events and real-time combat.

Decided The galaxy core has collapsed into a super black hole. The player leads a small squad of scout ships toward the far rim of the galaxy and must find a way, possibly a technology, to jump to a new galaxy.

Key differences from FTL

  • A squad of 1–6 player ships instead of a single ship.
  • Combat is squad against squad, with up to 6 enemy vessels.
  • An expanding collapse replaces the pursuing rebel fleet; escaping the galaxy replaces the flagship battle.
  • Built for multiplayer, up to one player per ship.

2. The Collapse & Campaign Structure

Proposed The collapse horizon expands outward over the run.

Proposed Event themes depend on distance from the horizon: close to it, events reflect the shock of the collapse and people scrambling to leave their planets; far from it, distant worlds may not yet believe the collapse is real.

Open Map structure: sectors with wider, galaxy-shaped paths outward, or no sectors and large node maps.

3. Squad Combat

Proposed Player squad on the bottom half of the screen, enemy squad on the top half.

Proposed Selecting a ship shows its power levels and systems near that ship, rather than in fixed screen positions, since there are several ships on screen.

Open Ship size and zoom needed to fit up to 12 ships on screen.

4. Multiplayer

Proposed Additional players join via a second controller or over LAN and issue orders to the ships assigned to them, up to 6 players, one per ship.

5. Ships

Proposed Ships are built from art + floor art + room layout + blueprint, as in FTL.

Proposed A room may hold more than one system.

Proposed Hull breaches can occur in walls, not only floors.

6. Jump Travel

Open How jumping works and what it is called: slipstream, system gates, hyperdrive, or something original. Possibly a different method per race.

10. Art Direction

Proposed Art is made as scalable vector graphics (SVG).

Open A visual identity that stands apart from FTL.

11. Modding & Data Format

Proposed Each ship is a self-contained folder (or zipped folder) holding every file it needs; adding a ship means dropping in its folder.

Proposed Weapons, and possibly other content, follow the same pattern: e.g. a folder in Weapons/ containing its icon art, ship-mounted art, and a data file labelled as a weapon.

12. Prototypes & Mockups

Proposed Small JavaScript demos built on art mockups to try ideas before committing to them, e.g. the per-ship power display.
